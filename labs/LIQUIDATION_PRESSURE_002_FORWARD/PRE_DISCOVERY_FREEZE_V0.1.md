## Mechanism

Forced deleveraging is mechanically generated by exchange liquidation engines when margin requirements are breached. A sufficiently concentrated burst of forced executions may create a temporary imbalance in:
- aggressive trade flow;
- top-of-book depth;
- spread;
- replenishment speed;
- short-horizon continuation or reversal.

The economic question is not "liquidations predict price" in general. It is whether an objectively frozen liquidation-pressure state produces a repeatable, accessible post-event microstructure response after costs.

## Event construction — frozen before outcomes

A liquidation event record is the raw exchange message containing:
- exchange event timestamp;
- liquidation update timestamp;
- symbol;
- liquidated position side;
- executed size;
- bankruptcy price.

A liquidation **burst** is evaluated in fixed non-overlapping event-time windows:
- 1 second
- 5 seconds
- 30 seconds

For each symbol/window record:
- liquidation notional proxy = sum(size × bankruptcy_price);
- long-liquidation notional;
- short-liquidation notional;
- signed imbalance = short_liq_notional - long_liq_notional.

No percentile/threshold is selected after price outcomes are opened. Threshold selection, if required for Discovery, must be frozen from source-only distributional data first.
